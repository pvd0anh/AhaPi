## What Is a Ratio?

A **ratio** compares two quantities of the same kind. If a group has $a$ items of one type and $b$ items of another, we say the ratio is $a$ **to** $b$.

### Three ways to write one ratio

| Form | How to read it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Colon | "3 to 4" | $3:4$ |
| Fraction | "3 over 4" | $\frac{3}{4}$ |
| Words | "3 to 4" | 3 to 4 |

All three mean exactly the same thing. Choose whichever is easiest to work with.

## Order Matters

A ratio is not just a pair of numbers — the **order** carries meaning. If a basket holds 3 apples and 4 oranges:

- apples to oranges is $3:4$
- oranges to apples is $4:3$

These are different statements. Always read the sentence carefully to see which quantity is named first.

> **Remember:** the first number in the ratio belongs to the first thing mentioned.

## Part to Part, Part to Whole

With 3 apples and 4 oranges there are $3+4=7$ fruits in total.

| Comparison | Ratio |
|---|---|
| apples to oranges (part to part) | $3:4$ |
| apples to all fruit (part to whole) | $3:7$ |
| oranges to all fruit (part to whole) | $4:7$ |

A part-to-whole ratio can also be read as a fraction of the total: apples make up $\frac{3}{7}$ of the basket.

[[BÀI TẬP]]

[[Câu]] A box contains 5 red pens and 8 blue pens. Write the ratio of red pens to blue pens in all three forms.
[[Đáp án]] $5:8$, or $\frac{5}{8}$, or "5 to 8".

[[Câu]] Using the same box, what is the ratio of blue pens to the total number of pens?
[[Đáp án]] The total is $5+8=13$ pens, so the ratio is $8:13$. Blue pens make up $\frac{8}{13}$ of the box.

[[Câu]] A class has 12 girls and 15 boys. A student writes "the ratio of boys to girls is $12:15$". Explain the mistake and give the correct answer.
[[Đáp án]] The question asks for **boys first**, so the numbers are in the wrong order. The correct ratio is $15:12$. The student wrote the ratio of girls to boys instead.
