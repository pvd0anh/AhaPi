This lesson is a practical tutorial for reading ratios off a picture. A ratio compares two quantities, written $a:b$ or as the fraction $\frac{a}{b}$. The video works through three examples.

* **Monkeys and Bananas (0:04 - 0:55):** Counting the items gives 4 monkeys and 5 bananas, so the ratio is $4:5$, or $\frac{4}{5}$.
* **Rock Collection (0:56 - 2:16):** Donna's collection has 6 pieces of granite, 3 of marble and 14 of sandstone. The ratio of sandstone to marble is $14:3$, or $\frac{14}{3}$.
* **Dinner Time (2:16 - 3:37):** Daphne has 12 pieces in her bowl and 7 pieces in her hair. The ratio of bowl to hair is $12:7$, or $\frac{12}{7}$.

## The Method

Every one of these follows the same three steps.

1. Read the question and note **which quantity is named first**.
2. Count that quantity, then count the second one.
3. Write the first count, a colon, then the second count.

> **Watch out:** a problem often gives you more numbers than you need. In the rock example there are three kinds of rock, but the question only asks about two of them — granite is not part of the answer.
