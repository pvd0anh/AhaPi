## Expanding a Ratio

**Expanding** means multiplying both parts of a ratio by the same number. The comparison does not change — only the size of the numbers does.

$$a:b \;=\; (a \times k):(b \times k) \qquad k \neq 0$$

Starting from $2:3$ and multiplying by $k=4$:

$$2:3 \;=\; 8:12$$

Both describe the same relationship. For every 2 of the first thing there are 3 of the second; equally, for every 8 there are 12.

### Why it works

Write the ratio as a fraction. Multiplying the top and the bottom by the same number gives an equivalent fraction:

$$\frac{2}{3}=\frac{2\times 4}{3\times 4}=\frac{8}{12}$$

> **Key idea:** whatever you do to one side of a ratio, do to the other. Multiply both, or divide both — never just one.

## When Expanding Is Useful

Expanding lets you scale a recipe or a plan up to the size you actually need.

| Situation | Ratio | Multiply by | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recipe for 2 people, cooking for 6 | $1:2$ | $3$ | $3:6$ |
| Model car, 1 cm stands for 20 cm | $1:20$ | $5$ | $5:100$ |

[[EXERCISES]]

[[Q]] Expand the ratio $3:5$ by a factor of 6.
[[A]] Multiply both parts: $3\times 6=18$ and $5\times 6=30$, giving $18:30$.

[[Q]] A paint mix uses blue and white in the ratio $2:7$. You need 21 litres of white. How much blue do you need?
[[A]] To get from 7 to 21 you multiply by 3, so multiply both parts by 3: $2:7=6:21$. You need **6 litres of blue**.

[[Q]] Is $4:6$ the same ratio as $10:15$? Justify your answer.
[[A]] Yes. Divide $4:6$ by 2 to get $2:3$, and divide $10:15$ by 5 to also get $2:3$. Both reduce to the same simplest form, so they are equivalent.
