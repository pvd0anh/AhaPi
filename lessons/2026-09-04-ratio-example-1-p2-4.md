## Finding a Missing Quantity

Part 2 of the ratio series moves from *reading* ratios to *using* them. The typical question gives you a ratio and one real amount, then asks for the other amount.

### The scale factor method

$$\text{scale factor}=\frac{\text{amount you know}}{\text{matching part of the ratio}}$$

Multiply the other part of the ratio by that same scale factor.

**Worked example.** Red and green marbles are in the ratio $3:5$. There are 12 red marbles. How many green?

1. The known amount, 12, matches the part $3$.
2. Scale factor: $\dfrac{12}{3}=4$.
3. Green marbles: $5\times 4=20$.

$$3:5 \;=\; 12:20$$

> **Check your answer:** divide both numbers of your final ratio by their common factor. If you get back the original ratio, you scaled correctly.

## A Second Route: Equivalent Fractions

The same problem as an equation, with $x$ for the unknown:

$$\frac{3}{5}=\frac{12}{x}$$

Cross multiply: $3x = 5 \times 12 = 60$, so $x = 20$. Same answer, different bookkeeping — use whichever you find clearer.

[[EXERCISES]]

[[Q]] Cats and dogs at a shelter are in the ratio $4:7$. There are 28 dogs. How many cats?
[[A]] The scale factor is $\dfrac{28}{7}=4$, so cats $=4\times 4=16$. Check: $16:28$ divides by 4 to give $4:7$. ✓

[[Q]] A recipe mixes flour and sugar in the ratio $5:2$. You use 250 g of flour. How much sugar?
[[A]] Scale factor $=\dfrac{250}{5}=50$, so sugar $=2\times 50=100$ g.

[[Q]] The ratio of boys to girls in a club is $2:3$. There are 18 girls. How many children are in the club altogether?
[[A]] Scale factor $=\dfrac{18}{3}=6$, so boys $=2\times 6=12$. The club has $12+18=30$ children.
