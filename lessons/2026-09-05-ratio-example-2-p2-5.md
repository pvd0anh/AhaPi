## Sharing a Total in a Given Ratio

This is the second big use of ratios: you know the **total**, and you split it into parts.

### The unit method

1. Add the parts of the ratio to get the number of **shares**.
2. Divide the total by the number of shares to find the value of one share.
3. Multiply each part of the ratio by the value of one share.

**Worked example.** Share 45 sweets between Ann and Ben in the ratio $4:5$.

$$\text{shares}=4+5=9 \qquad \text{one share}=\frac{45}{9}=5$$

$$\text{Ann}=4\times 5=20 \qquad \text{Ben}=5\times 5=25$$

Check: $20+25=45$. ✓

> **Always check that your parts add back to the total.** It catches almost every arithmetic slip.

## Telling the Two Question Types Apart

| The question gives you | What to do |
|---|---|
| The ratio and **one part** | Scale factor method (previous lesson) |
| The ratio and **the total** | Unit method (this lesson) |

Reading which of the two you have is the hardest step. "Share 45 sweets" gives a total; "there are 12 red marbles" gives one part.

[[BÀI TẬP]]

[[Câu]] Share 60 marbles between two players in the ratio $2:3$.
[[Đáp án]] Shares: $2+3=5$. One share: $\dfrac{60}{5}=12$. So the players get $2\times12=24$ and $3\times12=36$. Check: $24+36=60$. ✓

[[Câu]] A 84 cm ribbon is cut in the ratio $3:4$. How long is each piece?
[[Đáp án]] Shares: $3+4=7$. One share: $\dfrac{84}{7}=12$ cm. The pieces are $36$ cm and $48$ cm.

[[Câu]] Money is split between three people in the ratio $1:2:5$, and the total is 96. How much does each person get, and what fraction of the total does the last person receive?
[[Đáp án]] Shares: $1+2+5=8$. One share: $\dfrac{96}{8}=12$. The amounts are $12$, $24$ and $60$. The last person gets $\dfrac{5}{8}$ of the total.
