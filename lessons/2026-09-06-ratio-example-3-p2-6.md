## When the Question Gives You a Difference

A harder variation: instead of a total or a single part, the problem tells you **how much more** one quantity is than the other.

**Worked example.** Two numbers are in the ratio $3:7$, and the larger exceeds the smaller by 20. Find both numbers.

The difference in ratio parts is $7-3=4$ shares, and those 4 shares are worth 20.

$$\text{one share}=\frac{20}{4}=5$$

$$\text{smaller}=3\times 5=15 \qquad \text{larger}=7\times 5=35$$

Check: $35-15=20$. ✓

> **The pattern is always the same.** Work out how many shares the given information is worth, divide to find one share, then build every quantity from it.

## The Three Question Types Side by Side

| You are told | Shares it is worth | One share |
|---|---|---|
| One part, e.g. the $3$ part is 12 | $3$ | $\frac{12}{3}$ |
| The total, e.g. 45 in ratio $4:5$ | $4+5=9$ | $\frac{45}{9}$ |
| The difference, e.g. 20 in ratio $3:7$ | $7-3=4$ | $\frac{20}{4}$ |

Once you can name which row you are on, every ratio problem becomes the same problem.

[[BÀI TẬP]]

[[Câu]] Two numbers are in the ratio $2:5$ and differ by 18. Find them.
[[Đáp án]] Difference in shares: $5-2=3$. One share: $\dfrac{18}{3}=6$. The numbers are $12$ and $30$. Check: $30-12=18$. ✓

[[Câu]] In a car park the ratio of cars to vans is $9:4$. There are 45 more cars than vans. How many vehicles are there in total?
[[Đáp án]] Difference in shares: $9-4=5$, so one share $=\dfrac{45}{5}=9$. Cars $=81$, vans $=36$, total $=117$.

[[Câu]] Sam and Lily share stickers in the ratio $5:8$. Lily has 12 more than Sam. How many does Sam have?
[[Đáp án]] Difference in shares: $8-5=3$, so one share $=\dfrac{12}{3}=4$. Sam has $5\times 4=20$ stickers.
