## Subtracting Decimals

The rule is the same as for addition: **line up the decimal points**, then subtract column by column from the right.

**Worked example.** $15.7 - 8.24$

Pad the shorter number with a zero so both have two decimal places:

$$\begin{array}{r} 15.70 \\ -\ 8.24 \\ \hline 7.46 \end{array}$$

Working right to left:

1. Hundredths: $0 - 4$ is not possible, so borrow from the tenths. The $7$ becomes $6$ and the $0$ becomes $10$. Now $10-4=6$.
2. Tenths: $6-2=4$.
3. Ones: $15-8=7$.

## Why Padding Is Safe

Adding zeros to the **right** of the last decimal digit never changes the value:

$$15.7 = 15.70 = 15.700$$

Adding a zero anywhere else does change it — $15.07$ is a different number entirely.

> **Check by adding back.** If $15.70 - 8.24 = 7.46$, then $7.46 + 8.24$ should return $15.70$. It does.

[[Câu]] Explain why $3.5$ and $3.50$ are equal, but $3.5$ and $3.05$ are not.
[[Đáp án]] In $3.50$ the extra zero sits in the hundredths place and contributes nothing, so the value is unchanged. In $3.05$ the zero sits in the **tenths** place, pushing the 5 into the hundredths: $3.05$ means $3+\frac{5}{100}$, while $3.5$ means $3+\frac{5}{10}$.
