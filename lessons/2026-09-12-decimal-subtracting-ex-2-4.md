## Subtracting With Repeated Borrowing

A harder subtraction, where the borrowing chains across several columns.

**Worked example.** $30.02 - 17.485$

Pad the first number to three decimal places: $30.020$.

$$\begin{array}{r} 30.020 \\ -\ 17.485 \\ \hline 12.535 \end{array}$$

The thousandths column needs $0-5$, and the hundredths above it is also $2$, then $0$ — so the borrow travels several places left before it finds a digit to take from. Work patiently one column at a time and let the borrow propagate.

> **Always verify a long subtraction by adding your answer back to the number you subtracted.** Here $12.535 + 17.485 = 30.020$. ✓

## Subtracting From a Whole Number

Whole numbers hide their zeros, which is where mistakes creep in.

| Written | Think of it as |
|---|---|
| $8 - 3.6$ | $8.0 - 3.6 = 4.4$ |
| $50 - 12.75$ | $50.00 - 12.75 = 37.25$ |
| $1 - 0.001$ | $1.000 - 0.001 = 0.999$ |

[[EXERCISES]]

[[Q]] Work out $40.1 - 26.375$.
[[A]] Write $40.100 - 26.375 = 13.725$. Check: $13.725 + 26.375 = 40.100$. ✓

[[Q]] Work out $100 - 3.06$.
[[A]] Write $100.00 - 3.06 = 96.94$.

[[Q]] A bottle holds $1.5$ litres. After pouring out $0.85$ litres, then a further $0.4$ litres, how much is left?
[[A]] Poured out in total: $0.85 + 0.40 = 1.25$ litres. Remaining: $1.50 - 1.25 = 0.25$ litres.
