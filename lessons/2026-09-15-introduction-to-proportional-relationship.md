## What Makes a Relationship Proportional?

Two quantities $x$ and $y$ are in a **proportional relationship** when one is always the same number of times the other:

$$y = kx$$

The fixed number $k$ is the same for every pair of values. Equivalently, the ratio $\frac{y}{x}$ never changes.

**Example.** Pencils cost 3 each.

| Pencils $x$ | Cost $y$ | $\frac{y}{x}$ |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 6 | 3 |
| 5 | 15 | 3 |
| 8 | 24 | 3 |

The last column is constant, so the relationship is proportional with $k=3$, and $y=3x$.

## How to Recognise One

A relationship is proportional when **all three** of these hold:

1. The ratio $\frac{y}{x}$ is the same for every pair.
2. Doubling $x$ doubles $y$; tripling $x$ triples $y$.
3. The graph is a straight line **through the origin** $(0,0)$.

> **The origin matters.** A straight-line graph is not enough. If a taxi charges a fixed 5 to start plus 2 per km, the graph is straight but starts at $(0,5)$ — that is $y=2x+5$, which is **not** proportional, because $\frac{y}{x}$ keeps changing.

## Counter-example Worth Remembering

| $x$ | $y$ | $\frac{y}{x}$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7 | 7 |
| 2 | 9 | 4.5 |
| 3 | 11 | 3.67 |

The ratio changes, so this is not proportional — even though $y$ rises steadily.

[[EXERCISES]]

[[Q]] A table shows $x=4, y=10$ and $x=6, y=15$. Is the relationship proportional? If so, give $k$.
[[A]] $\frac{10}{4}=2.5$ and $\frac{15}{6}=2.5$. The ratio is constant, so yes — the relationship is proportional with $k=2.5$, and $y=2.5x$.

[[Q]] A gym charges a 20 joining fee plus 15 per month. Is the total cost proportional to the number of months? Explain.
[[A]] No. The cost is $y=15x+20$, so for 1 month $\frac{35}{1}=35$ but for 2 months $\frac{50}{2}=25$. The ratio changes and the graph misses the origin.

[[Q]] If $y$ is proportional to $x$ and $y=18$ when $x=4$, find $y$ when $x=10$.
[[A]] $k=\frac{18}{4}=4.5$, so $y=4.5x$. When $x=10$, $y=45$.
