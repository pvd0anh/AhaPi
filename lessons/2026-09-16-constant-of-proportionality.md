## The Constant of Proportionality

In $y = kx$, the number $k$ is called the **constant of proportionality**. You find it by dividing:

$$k = \frac{y}{x}$$

It answers the question: *how much $y$ do I get for one unit of $x$?* That is why it is often called the **unit rate**.

**Worked example.** A car travels 210 km in 3 hours at a steady speed.

$$k=\frac{210}{3}=70$$

So $y = 70x$, and the constant 70 is the speed in km per hour.

## Reading $k$ From Different Places

| Given | How to find $k$ |
|---|---|
| A table of values | Divide any $y$ by its $x$ |
| The equation $y=kx$ | Read the coefficient directly |
| A graph | $k$ is the height of the line at $x=1$ |
| A word problem | Find the amount for **one** unit |

> **$k$ carries units.** In the car example $k$ is not just "70" — it is 70 kilometres **per hour**. Naming the units turns a bare number into something you can interpret.

## Watch the Direction

Swapping which quantity is $x$ gives the reciprocal constant. From the same car:

$$\text{km per hour}=\frac{210}{3}=70 \qquad \text{hours per km}=\frac{3}{210}=\frac{1}{70}$$

Both are correct; they answer different questions.

[[BÀI TẬP]]

[[Câu]] 5 notebooks cost 40. Find the constant of proportionality and write the equation.
[[Đáp án]] $k=\frac{40}{5}=8$ per notebook, so $y=8x$.

[[Câu]] A proportional graph passes through $(6, 27)$. What is $k$, and what is $y$ when $x=14$?
[[Đáp án]] $k=\frac{27}{6}=4.5$, so $y=4.5x$. When $x=14$, $y=63$.

[[Câu]] A printer produces 90 pages in 4 minutes. Give the constant of proportionality both as pages per minute and as minutes per page.
[[Đáp án]] Pages per minute: $\frac{90}{4}=22.5$. Minutes per page: $\frac{4}{90}=\frac{2}{45}\approx 0.044$. They are reciprocals of each other.
