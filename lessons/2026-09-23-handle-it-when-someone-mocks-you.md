## Why This Lesson Sits in a Maths Course

Learning maths means being wrong in public — a wrong answer on the board, a question that sounds obvious to everyone else. Students who fear being laughed at stop asking, and students who stop asking stop learning. Handling mockery is therefore a study skill, not a side topic.

## Three Reactions, Three Outcomes

| Reaction | What it looks like | What usually follows |
|---|---|---|
| Fight back | Insult in return | The exchange escalates; both look bad |
| Shrink | Go silent, avoid the class | The fear grows and the learning stops |
| Stay steady | Acknowledge briefly, carry on | The comment loses its power |

The third response is a skill, and like any skill it improves with practice.

## A Short Script That Works

1. **Pause.** Take one breath before answering. Nothing is lost in a second of silence.
2. **Name it plainly, without heat.** "That was a put-down." Describing the behaviour is stronger than defending yourself.
3. **Return to the task.** "Anyway — can someone explain step two?" Redirecting shows the comment did not derail you.
4. **Tell an adult if it repeats.** A single joke is a moment; a pattern is bullying, and that is not yours to fix alone.

> **The point is not to win the exchange.** It is to keep your attention on your own work. Someone who mocks a question is telling you about themselves, not about your ability.

## When You Are the Bystander

Silence reads as agreement. A short sentence — "That wasn't funny, let them finish" — costs little and changes the room more than anything the target can say.
